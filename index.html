KEYNOTE ADDRESSES

Friction Worth Keeping: What Keeps Us Human When AI Makes Everything Easy

Andrew Heiss, Ph.D., Assistant Professor of International Politics and Policy, Georgetown University in Qatar

Opening Keynote · November 10 at 8:00 AM, Eastern

Generative AI removes friction. Need a poster for an event? Slides for stakeholders? An R package to access an API? An answer for a problem set? It’s all a prompt away. But not all friction is bad. Some friction is formative and central to the human experience: struggling to learn develops skills and expertise, and working through hard problems builds understanding and judgment. How can we tell what friction is worth keeping?

Beyond the prompt

Mine Çetinkaya-Rundel, Associate Director of Undergraduate Studies & Professor of the Practice, Department of Statistical Science, Duke University; Director of the First-Year Experience, Trinity College of Arts & Sciences

Closing Keynote · November 11 at 3:15 PM, Eastern

AI tools can generate a polished ggplot, scaffold a Quarto report, and debug a dplyr pipeline in seconds. Should students still learn to do these things themselves? I argue yes, and that teaching programming and reproducible workflows is more important than ever. This talk reframes programming as a pathway to statistical thinking rather than syntax acquisition, and explores assignments and assessments that prepare students to use AI critically, reproducibly, and responsibly.
